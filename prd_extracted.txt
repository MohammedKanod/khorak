PS000052 —  Khorak : Supplementary Nutrition Supply Chain Tracker Oct 15, 2026 · @Mohammed  kanod Executive summary Khorak  is a mobile-first, offline-capable supply chain tracker that closes the accountability gap between food delivery and child nutrition in Gujarat's 53,000+ Anganwadi  Centres  — a gap the CAG documented in 2024 and the DDO  Panchmahal  formalized as PS000052 for SFIC 2026. The problem in one line:  India's supplementary nutrition system has a confirmed delivery mechanism (GCMMFL's OTP for THR) but no system to verify what happens after delivery — whether food is correctly stored, distributed to the right beneficiaries, in the right quantities, on the right days. The result: 14% of enrolled beneficiaries (64 lakh people across Gujarat alone) are not receiving their entitled nutrition, and no one in the chain knows exactly where the food is going. What  Khorak  does:  It gives every Anganwadi Worker a simple, Gujarati-language, offline-first app to log stock intake, daily distribution, spoilage, and physical counts in under 2 minutes per interaction — then auto-generates the Monthly Progress Report that currently takes an entire pre-sector meeting to compile manually. Supervisors and CDPOs get a real-time sector dashboard with exception alerts, delivery verification, and anomaly detection across their AWCs. Competition context:  PS000052 under the Seva First Innovation Challenge 2026, set by the District Development Office,  Panchmahal . The problem setter is a Gujarat district authority, making local impact the primary judging lens. The solution is positioned as the missing accountability layer that complements — not competes with — the existing  Poshan  Tracker, which tracks beneficiary health outcomes but has no supply chain module. Why this will win:  Grounded in a 2024 CAG audit of Gujarat's own ICDS system (same state, real numbers), targets a documented gap that three prior government digital initiatives failed to close, and demos a working prototype that eliminates the single most hated administrative task of every AWW: end-of-month register compilation. Problem context What is ICDS and why it matters The Integrated Child Development Services scheme, launched in 1975, is the world's largest early childhood  programme . In Gujarat alone it operates through 53,029 Anganwadi  Centres  serving 5.53 crore people. The Supplementary Nutrition  Programme  (SNP) under ICDS is legally mandated to provide nutrition to children aged 0–6 years, pregnant women, lactating mothers, and adolescent girls for 300 days a year. It delivered on that target on average only 246 days per year across 2015–2023 in Gujarat — 82% of the mandate. 14% of enrolled beneficiaries (64 lakh people) received no benefit at all. The administrative hierarchy The ICDS system operates through a strict five-tier hierarchy: Level Role Scope National Ministry of Women & Child Development (MWCD) Policy and funding State WCD Department, Gujarat Implementation oversight District District  Programme  Officer (DPO) All blocks in the district Block Child Development Project Officer (CDPO) 75–150 AWCs per block Sector Supervisor ( Mukhya  Sevika) 25–30 AWCs per sector Centre Anganwadi Worker (AWW) + Helper (AWH) 1 AWC serving ~800–1,000 population The AWW is the sole frontline worker — she runs the  centre , distributes food, monitors children, maintains all records, and submits all reports. She currently carries 13 paper registers, each 50 pages thick, covering every ICDS service. The supply chain: two tracks Hot Cooked Meal (HCM)  serves children aged 3–6 years at the  centre . Raw material (wheat, rice, dal, oil) flows from FCI  godowns  → GSCSCL lifts and stores → distributed to AWCs via Fair Price Shops → AWW or SHG cooks and serves. Take Home Ration (THR)  serves children aged 0–3 years, pregnant women, lactating mothers, and adolescent girls. Packaged premix flows from GCMMFL manufacturing → GCMMFL delivery personnel → AWC storeroom → beneficiary home. Gujarat introduced an OTP verification system for this leg in January 2020: the delivery person shares an OTP which the AWW enters to confirm receipt. This is the only digital touchpoint currently in the entire supply chain. A day in the AWW's life (relevant to this solution) The  centre  is open 9:30 am to 2:00 pm. After children leave, the AWW spends the remaining afternoon on home visits and register updates. Monthly, she must compile all daily register entries into a Monthly Progress Report (MPR) — a process so laborious that workers hold a dedicated 'pre-sector meeting' just to help each other fill it in correctly. This is the primary administrative pain point  Khorak  eliminates. Ground-level research findings For the Anganwadi Worker How nutrition supplies are currently recorded:  Everything is paper. The AWW maintains a  Poshahar  register, a stock register, and a distribution register requiring daily entries. Distribution entries require the beneficiary's signature or thumb impression beside the quantity given. For THR specifically, Gujarat's OTP system (GCMMFL, since January 2020) provides one digital confirmation — but it only covers the moment of delivery, not what happens after. When physical stock doesn't match the recorded balance:  There is no formal reconciliation process. Discrepancies are either absorbed into the next month's figures or verbally flagged at the next sector meeting. No correction workflow, no write-off log, no audit trail. CAG audits have repeatedly found short supplies 'remaining unaccounted for' because no acknowledgement receipts or stock registers established full delivery. The most time-consuming task:  Monthly report compilation. AWWs transfer data from 13 separate registers onto the MPR form. The process is so complex that a 'pre-sector meeting' — where 30 AWWs sit together to help each other fill in correct numbers — has become a permanent institution. A 2020 peer-reviewed study confirmed AWWs spend substantial time on administrative tasks, directly cutting into child-facing activities. Offline vs  Poshan  Tracker:   Poshan  Tracker has an offline mode (sync within 5 days) but as of August 2025 reporting, only 1 in 6 AWWs surveyed used the app themselves — others had family members enter data, or phones had stopped working. Only 8.5% of Indian districts have eliminated paper registers. Critically,  Poshan  Tracker does not track stock intake, distribution quantities per household, or supply chain movement. That is the white space  Khorak  occupies. For the Supervisor / CDPO What they review and how often:  Supervisors are responsible for 25–30 AWCs and are supposed to make monthly physical visits. In Gujarat, 14.35% of Lady Supervisor positions were vacant as of the 2024 CAG audit — meaning active supervisors cover far more  centres . CDPOs review consolidated MPRs, not individual AWC stock data, unless a specific complaint is escalated. How shortages are identified today:  Entirely reactive. The AWW verbally reports at the next sector meeting, the supervisor notices during a physical visit, or beneficiaries complain. There is no proactive digital alert system. A CAG audit of Odisha found short delivery of 62,686 quintals of rice across sampled AWCs — discovered only during the audit, not in real time. Who can correct stock records:  No  standardised  policy exists. In practice, write-offs for spoilage or rodent damage are either not recorded or informally noted. The CAG Gujarat 2024 audit found THR packets damaged by rodents in 11 AWCs due to inadequate storage — none of these incidents had formal write-off records. Most useful alerts:  Based on CAG recommendations and field research: AWCs with no stock update in 5+ days, deliveries acknowledged at less than 80% of expected quantity, stock projected to run out before the next delivery cycle, and repeated spoilage from the same  centre . For the district / supply chain authority Where the main gap occurs:  The CAG Gujarat 2024 report (covering  Panchmahal  as one of eight sampled districts) identified three layers of failure: (1) the State lifted only 94% of  GoI -allocated foodgrains, letting 0.40 lakh MT lapse; (2) additional foodgrains needed for state-sponsored schemes were only 15–34% procured; (3) beneficiaries received nutrition on average 246 days/year vs the 300-day mandate. Crucially, data from DPO  Panchmahal  was not even available for the audit — meaning the district has no reliable AWC-level visibility. Key participants in the supply chain:   GoI  (FCI  godowns ) → Gujarat State Civil Supply Corporation Ltd (GSCSCL, lifts and stores) → Fair Price Shops (FPS, for HCM raw material) → AWC. For THR: GCMMFL manufactures premix packets → GCMMFL delivery personnel → AWC storeroom → beneficiary. The Vadodara municipality episode (2025) showed that when a local implementing body's payment certification fails, 400 AWCs can lose milk supply for 5,500 children with no early warning. Integration expectation:   Khorak  should fill the gap between  Poshan  Tracker (beneficiary-facing) and the GCMMFL OTP system (single delivery confirmation). It is not a replacement — it is the missing post-delivery accountability layer. The DDO  Panchmahal's  PS expects a supply chain tracking solution, not another beneficiary health app. Supply chain gaps and leakage points Three specific points in the ICDS supply chain are completely untracked today. Every feature of  Khorak  maps back to plugging one of these gaps. Gap 1 — District to AWC: unverified delivery quantity.  Suppliers dispatch food; AWWs receive it. There is no digital acknowledgement of how much actually arrived vs how much was ordered. The existing GCMMFL OTP confirms the delivery event but not the quantity. Short deliveries are never caught at time of receipt — only discovered during periodic audits, months later.  Khorak  closes this with QR-scanned intake + mandatory photo proof at delivery. Gap 2 — AWC storeroom: no running stock visibility.  Once food enters an AWC, it disappears from any tracked system until the monthly paper register is compiled. No supervisor, CDPO, or district officer has  live stock  visibility. Food can run out, spoil, or be misappropriated with no real-time signal.  Khorak  maintains a live running balance: Opening stock + Intakes − Distributions − Write-offs = Current stock. Automatically calculated, never requires manual entry. Gap 3 — AWC to beneficiary: no per-household distribution proof.  Distribution quantities are recorded per day in aggregate, not per beneficiary. There is no way to verify whether a specific child or pregnant woman received their entitled amount.  Poshan  Tracker records whether a beneficiary is enrolled — not whether they received food.  Khorak  adds per-beneficiary distribution logs with screen-signature or photo acknowledgment. Feature specifications F1 — Beneficiary roster setup Problem it solves:  Creating a digital beneficiary list from scratch requires hours of manual form-filling. Without a fast setup, AWWs will abandon the app on day one. How it works:  The supervisor or CDPO initiates roster creation for a new AWC. The AWW scans the QR code on the back of each beneficiary's Aadhaar card. The Aadhaar offline XML (readable without internet) pre-fills: name, date of birth, gender, and address. The system auto-assigns the beneficiary's entitlement category based on age: 0–3 years → THR, 3–6 years → HCM, pregnant/lactating → THR 600 kcal, adolescent girl → THR 600 kcal. The AWW confirms or adjusts the auto-assignment. Fallback for  beneficiaries without Aadhaar: voice dictation of name + manual age entry + manual category selection. Target: 40-beneficiary roster completable in under 60 minutes. OCR note:  Aadhaar QR scan is preferred over OCR for accuracy and reliability. OCR is available as a tertiary fallback for non-Aadhaar documents (old registers, challans). Phase 2:  Aadhaar biometric authentication (requires UIDAI license and STQC-certified device — documented as future upgrade, not built in Phase 1). F2 — Stock intake logging Problem it solves:  Deliveries are confirmed as events (GCMMFL OTP) but not as quantity-verified receipts. Short deliveries are never caught at time of arrival. How it works:  When a delivery arrives, the AWW opens the Intake screen. She scans the QR code on the delivery challan — the app pre-fills supplier name, item list, expected quantity, and batch number. She enters or confirms the actual quantity received (using large stepper +/– buttons — no keyboard). She takes a mandatory timestamped, GPS-tagged photo of the delivery and goods. The system sends an OTP to the supervisor, who confirms the delivery is logged. If actual quantity received is less than 85% of the expected quantity, the system auto-raises a short-delivery flag to the supervisor. The running stock balance updates automatically. Fallback:  If no QR is on the challan, the AWW selects the supplier from a dropdown and enters the challan number manually. F3 — Daily distribution logging Problem it solves:  Distribution quantities are recorded in aggregate, not per beneficiary. There is no proof that a specific child or mother received their entitled ration.  Poshan  Tracker records enrolment, not delivery of food. How it works (HCM — hot cooked meal):  The AWW opens today's attendance list. She taps each child present to mark attendance. After the meal, she enters the total HCM portions served. The app crosschecks: if portions served exceeds attendance by more than 5%, an over-reporting flag is raised. Stock balance decreases automatically by the calculated raw-material equivalent. How it works (THR — take home ration):  The AWW opens the THR distribution screen. Each beneficiary's name, photo (from Aadhaar), and current entitlement are shown. She taps each beneficiary as she hands them their packet. The beneficiary signs on screen (finger trace) or the AWW takes a photo. The packet count decrements from stock automatically. Distribution count toward the government-mandated 300-day target updates in real time. Input modes:  (1) Tap-select from beneficiary list, (2) QR scan from beneficiary's app profile card, (3) voice dictation for notes. No typing required for any core distribution action. F4 — Running stock balance engine Problem it solves:  There is no  live stock  visibility. The only way to know how much food is at an AWC today is to physically visit it. How it works:  The stock balance for each item is calculated as: Current balance = Opening stock + Σ(Intakes) − Σ(Distributions) −  Σ( Approved write-offs) This recalculates automatically after every logged event. The AWW sees a simple stock level indicator (Full / Adequate / Low / Critical) for each item. No manual stock entry is ever required — the balance is always the mathematical result of logged transactions. Monthly physical count reconciliation:  Once a month, the AWW does a physical count. She enters actual quantities found. The app computes the discrepancy vs calculated balance and raises a reconciliation flag. The flag flows to the supervisor for review — it is not an  accusation,  it is a request for explanation. Possible reason codes: measurement error, unreported spillage, delivery not yet logged, theft/diversion. F5 — Spoilage and write-off reporting Problem it solves:  Spoilage (rodent damage, moisture, expiry) currently goes unrecorded. CAG Gujarat 2024 found rodent-damaged THR packets in 11 AWCs with no documentation. How it works:  The AWW presses the 'Report spoilage' button. She records a voice note describing what happened (transcribed automatically) and photographs the damaged goods. She selects a reason code: Rodent damage / Moisture / Expired before distribution / Quality issue on arrival / Other. She estimates the quantity affected using stepper buttons. The flag is submitted to the supervisor with timestamp and GPS. Approval thresholds:  Write-off < 500g (or equivalent units) → supervisor approves. Write-off ≥ 500g → CDPO approval required. Every approved write-off is immutably logged with the approver's identity, timestamp, and reason. District-level value:  Reason codes aggregate across all AWCs. CDPOs and DPOs can identify which supplier's stock has the highest spoilage rate, and which AWCs have storage problems requiring infrastructure improvement. F6 — Predictive stock alerts Problem it solves:  Stock runs out before the next delivery, with no warning. AWWs discover the shortage only when there is nothing left to serve. How it works:  After 30 days of distribution data, the system calculates the rolling daily consumption rate per item per AWC. It compares current balance against the projected consumption until the next scheduled delivery date. If projected runout date is earlier than delivery date, the AWW receives: 'Rice will run out in 8 days. Next delivery expected in 14 days. Tap to draft an indent.' The auto-drafted indent pre-fills item name, current balance, projected shortfall quantity, and delivery address. The AWW sends it to the supervisor in one tap. Risk  note :  Predictions require at least 30 days of data to be reliable. For the competition demo, show the algorithm design using mock historical data and demonstrate the alert triggering. F7 — Auto-generated Monthly Progress Report (MPR) Problem it solves:  Monthly report compilation currently requires a full pre-sector meeting where 30 AWWs sit together to help each other fill in correct numbers from their 13 registers. This is the most time-consuming administrative task in the entire AWW's month. How it works:  On or after the last day of each month, the AWW taps 'Generate MPR.' The app compiles: opening stock per item, total received, total distributed per beneficiary category, closing stock, total wastage/write-offs, number of distribution days (vs the 300-day mandate), and any pending discrepancy flags. The report is generated as a PDF in the standard government MPR format. The AWW reviews, signs digitally, and submits to the supervisor in one tap. Poshan  Tracker compatibility:  The distribution-days data and beneficiary coverage data from the MPR is exported in a format compatible with  Poshan  Tracker's reporting module, so AWWs do not have to enter the same data twice. F8 — Supervisor sector dashboard and alerts Problem it solves:  Supervisors have no between-visit visibility into the 25–30 AWCs in their sector. Problems are discovered at monthly visits or sector meetings — weeks after they begin. How it works:  The supervisor's home screen shows a heatmap of her sector: each AWC as a  coloured  card — green (all good), amber (attention needed), red (critical). Tapping any card shows that AWC's stock levels, last update time, and any open flags. Four alert triggers (push notifications): Delivery unacknowledged 48 hours after scheduled date Any item's stock falls below the threshold (7-day supply) No distribution entry logged for 3 or more consecutive days Physical count discrepancy exceeds 10% of calculated balance Verification workflow:  When an AWW logs an intake with a quantity mismatch (short delivery), the supervisor receives a verification task. She can approve the logged quantity, correct it, or schedule a physical visit. Every verification action is timestamped. F9 — CDPO delivery schedule and supplier module Problem it solves:  The 'delivery unacknowledged' alert (F8) has no trigger date unless the CDPO enters when deliveries are planned. Also, without a supplier dispatch log, there is no way to know if a delivery was ever dispatched. How it works (CDPO):  The CDPO enters delivery schedules: which AWCs receive delivery, on which dates, from which supplier, and for which items and quantities. This creates the expected delivery events that AWWs confirm against. How it works (Supplier/SHG):  The supplier logs dispatch: items dispatched, quantity, batch number, vehicle number, driver contact, and expected arrival time. The AWW's intake screen shows 'Delivery incoming today from GCMMFL — 45 THR packets expected' before the truck arrives. When the AWW completes intake, the supplier's dispatch record is marked delivered. F10 — Authorization hierarchy and audit log Problem it solves:  No formal correction process exists. Discrepancies are either ignored or informally fixed with no record. This enables fraud and also unfairly implicates honest AWWs. How it works:  Every data event — intake, distribution, spoilage flag, reconciliation, correction — is written to an immutable audit log. Fields: actor role, actor ID, AWC ID, timestamp, event type, item, quantity, previous value (for corrections), new value, reason code. No records are deleted. Every correction requires a reason code. Corrections above threshold require approval. The audit log is the single most important feature for judges:  It transforms the system from a data-entry tool into an accountability infrastructure. The CAG's audit findings exist precisely because no such log existed. F11 — Offline-first architecture and sync Problem it solves:  Rural AWCs frequently have no internet connectivity. Any solution that requires connectivity to function will be abandoned immediately. How it works:  All AWW-side writes go to a local SQLite database on the device first. Actions are queued in a sync queue. When connectivity is detected ( WiFi  or mobile data), the queue uploads to the server. Conflict resolution: last-write-wins with timestamp, flagging any conflicting edits for supervisor review. The app is fully functional with zero connectivity. Sync status is visible at the top of the screen (Last synced: 2  hrs  ago). F12 — Gujarati language and accessibility Problem it solves:  AWWs have low English literacy. Any English UI creates a barrier to adoption. Past government apps ( Poshan  Tracker) failed partly because of this. How it works:  Every screen, label, notification, and voice prompt is in Gujarati. Voice input uses a Gujarati speech-to-text model. Large-button stepper UI for quantity entry (no number keyboard). Icons paired with text for all actions. Font size configurable. Onboarding walkthrough in Gujarati with illustrated steps. F13 — Fallback input modes Problem it solves:  Not every interaction can use the primary input method. Barcodes may be missing, voice may not work in noisy environments, the AWW may be helping a child. Three input modes for every data entry point: QR / barcode scan  — preferred for intake (challan QR) and beneficiary identification (Aadhaar QR) Voice-to-text in Gujarati  — preferred for notes, spoilage descriptions, and any free-text field Large-button stepper UI  — preferred for quantity entry; +1 / −1 buttons with large display, no keyboard Every feature degrades gracefully through these modes if the primary fails. No feature is blocked by a missing QR or unavailable microphone. Key design decisions Beneficiary Aadhaar is stored as a one-way hash only  — the actual Aadhaar number never persists on the server. This satisfies data  minimisation  under PDPB 2023. All AWW writes are local-first  — SQLite on device, synced to server. Server records carry a  device_id  and  local_timestamp  alongside the server timestamp for conflict resolution. Soft deletes only  — no record in any table is ever physically deleted. Status fields and the audit log capture all state changes. Khorak  tracks the food itself, from the moment it leaves the supplier's vehicle to the moment it reaches the beneficiary's hands. These are two different systems solving two different problems.
